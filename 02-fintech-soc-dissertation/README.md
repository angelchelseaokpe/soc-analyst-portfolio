# Fintech Cybersecurity & SOC Effectiveness Research

## Project Overview

This research project formed part of my final-year BSc (Hons) Cyber Security degree and explored the relationship between remote working, cybersecurity risk and Security Operations Centre (SOC) effectiveness within fintech organisations.

The project developed across three stages:

1. Literature Review
2. Research Proposal
3. Final Dissertation

My interest in this topic came from my career focus on SOC and Blue Team cybersecurity and my interest in understanding how security controls perform in real operational environments.

---

## Research Focus

The final dissertation investigated how existing academic, industry and regulatory literature explains the impact of remote working on cybersecurity risks, defence strategies and SOC effectiveness in fintech organisations.

The research focused on areas including:

- Security Operations Centres (SOC)
- SIEM monitoring
- Alert triage
- Incident response
- Alert fatigue
- Mean Time to Detect (MTTD)
- Mean Time to Respond (MTTR)
- Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
- Virtual Private Networks (VPNs)
- Zero Trust
- Endpoint monitoring
- BYOD security
- Phishing and human factors

---

## Research Methodology

The final study used qualitative document analysis and reflexive thematic analysis to examine academic research, industry publications and regulatory literature.

The analysis focused on identifying patterns across remote-working cybersecurity risks, defence strategies and SOC effectiveness.

---

## Key Findings

The research identified six key themes:

1. Expanded attack surface in remote working
2. BYOD and endpoint security gaps
3. Phishing and human factors
4. Defence strategies between theory and practice
5. SOC visibility, alert fatigue and incident response
6. The under-represented SOC perspective

The research found that controls such as MFA, VPNs, Zero Trust, SIEM and endpoint monitoring are important, but their effectiveness depends on implementation quality, governance, analyst capability and integration with SOC workflows.

A key conclusion was that SOC effectiveness is an important link between cybersecurity controls and real operational security outcomes.
